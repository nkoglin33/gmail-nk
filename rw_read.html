• Rate headwinds pressure all four segments in the week ending October 9. The 10-year Treasury yield sustained its push above 5% through the week, dragging all REIT segments lower. Mall/Lifestyle led declines at ‑3.55% average — CBL reversed ‑11.32% on profit-taking after a strong YTD run; MAC fell ‑3.47% — while Grocery-Anchored held up best at ‑0.74% average with REG the sole positive large-cap name (+0.63%). REG’s Q2 guidance mid-point of ~$4.86 FFO/share implies earnings durability against cost-of-capital headwinds.
  • Brixmor-Slate deal prices grocery-anchored private market. Brixmor and Everview Partners agreed Sep 28 to acquire Slate Grocery REIT for $2.3B ($13.00/unit, ~20% premium to the Sep 23 close). Brixmor takes 23 centers (~3M sf, ~96% leased, primarily FL/GA/Carolinas) for $636M; the implied cap rate aligns with the ~6.7% national grocery-anchored benchmark. The deal validates institutional demand for the format even as public-market valuations compress under rate pressure.

Agree Realty (ADC) reports first; the grocery-anchored sprint runs October 26–November 5 — BRX and PECO (Oct 26–27), KIM/KRG/CURB (Oct 29), FRT (Oct 30), and REG (Nov 5). All Q3 figures currently unknown; all operating data in this report carries forward from Q2 2026. Watch for SP NOI guidance revisions given the interest-rate backdrop.
  • Analyst moves: KIM in the crosshairs. Morgan Stanley raised its Kimco Realty price target to $25 (from $24) and Barclays cut to $27 (from $28) within days of each other in late September, reflecting rate-driven mixed conviction even as KIM’s Q2 occupancy hit a record 96.4%. Wells Fargo carried an $88 Overweight on REG and UBS stood at $81 following the July 29 Q2 print.
  • Grocery-anchored supply gap widens. CBRE’s 2026 investor survey finds 85% of retail investors favor grocery-anchored centers — the most preferred retail format by a wide margin. New supply remains near-zero in most MSAs; Kroger, Publix and Walmart Neighborhood Market expansions continue to drive grocer-led leasing demand. Grocery-anchored transaction volume hit $11B in 2025 (JLL data, +42% YoY), reinforcing the format’s defensive positioning as the rate cycle peaks.
