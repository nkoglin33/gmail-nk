THIS WEEK'S READ

Power/diversified and convenience led a mixed week — Federal Realty (+2.1%) and Curbline (+1.8%) topped the tape on strong Q2 follow-through, while malls lagged badly as Macerich sold off nearly 7% and net lease drifted lower with the 10-year holding near 4.30%. The operating theme of Q2 season is now unmistakable: record occupancy and double-digit blended spreads across the strip universe, with Brixmor's 5.8% same-property NOI growth and Kimco's all-time-high 96.4% occupancy setting the pace. The public-private gap remains the trade — grocery-anchored assets are clearing privately at 5.25–5.5% caps while the public grocery peer set trades at a ~6.0% implied cap, roughly 55–75 bps wide, with institutional buyers pushing a record ~$12.8B of trailing-twelve-month grocery-anchored volume. For Regency, the read-through is constructive: our raised FY26 guidance ($4.84–$4.88 FFO), near-97% lease rate, and $680M development pipeline at 9% blended returns are being validated by both peer prints and private-market pricing that continues to mark grocery-anchored NAVs higher.

SEGMENT CROSS-SECTION

 

Convenience / Strip — Curbline's Record Deployment Quarter Sets the Pace

Curbline (+1.8% on the week) continues to earn its premium multiple, posting record Q2 investment volume of $375M and raising full-year OFFO guidance to $1.24–$1.26 while running effectively unlevered against a segment averaging 5x+ net debt/EBITDA. Phillips Edison consolidated after its strong print — 3.8% same-center NOI growth, record 97.3% leased occupancy, 95.5% record inline occupancy, and new lease spreads of 33.7% — with same-center NOI guidance tightened to 3.4–4.0%. Acadia beat on FFO ($0.31 vs. $0.30) on record street-retail spreads and guided to 10% FFO growth, though the stock remains the segment's YTD laggard. SITE Centers (wind-down) continues liquidating, selling The Pike Outlets and Meadowmont Market, with the equity now a $165M stub.

Read-through for Regency: Curbline's $375M quarter confirms aggressive institutional bidding for small-format convenience assets at sub-6% caps — direct competition for the shop-heavy end of our acquisition funnel, and a reason to lean on our development pipeline where we create equivalent assets at a 9% blended yield.

Grocery-Anchored — Brixmor's 5.8% SS NOI Print Headlines a Sweep of Guidance Raises

All four grocery names have now raised 2026 guidance. Brixmor led operationally with 5.8% same-property NOI growth and a record $71M signed-but-not-commenced ABR pipeline; Kimco matched its all-time-high 96.4% pro-rata occupancy with record 92.9% small-shop occupancy, 13.1% blended spreads (40.4% on new leases), and lifted FFO guidance to $1.83–$1.84. Regency delivered 3.8% same-property NOI growth, a near-97% lease rate, and raised FFO guidance to $4.84–$4.88. InvenTrust (+27.4% YTD) remains the segment's best performer on Sun Belt momentum. The week's price action was muted — the group is digesting a strong run, with implied caps steady around 6.0%.

Read-through for Regency: Peer prints validate the durability of grocery-anchored demand, but they also confirm every peer is chasing the same record occupancy math — sustaining our spread and SNO commencement schedule (69% of the $41M pipeline expected to commence by year-end) is the differentiator into 2027.

Power / Diversified — Federal Realty Breaks Out to the Top of the Universe

Federal Realty (+2.1% on the week, +29.0% YTD) touched the top of its 52-week range as investors reward its mixed-use durability and 58-year dividend growth streak. Urban Edge posted record FFO-as-adjusted growth of 10% year-over-year and raised AFFO guidance to $1.50–$1.54; Kite Realty printed 3.7% same-property NOI growth but still trades at the deepest NAV discount in the strip universe (~0.85x), keeping it perennial on activist and take-private screens. The segment's 6.3% average implied cap sits notably wide of grocery peers despite comparable operating metrics.

Read-through for Regency: KRG's persistent discount is the cautionary tale on format mix — the market pays for grocery-anchored predictability, not power-center GLA, reinforcing our merchandising discipline as centers turn over anchor boxes.

Mall / Lifestyle — Macerich Selloff Drags the Segment to the Bottom of the Tape

The mall complex was the week's clear laggard at −3.4%, driven by Macerich's near-7% slide as investors questioned the pace of its Path-Forward deleveraging from 7.9x net debt/EBITDA against softer NOI progression. Tanger fell 2.9% in sympathy despite intact fundamentals, while Simon held in (−0.5%), supported by its $87B scale, A-rated balance sheet, and continued premium-outlet strength. Even after the pullback, MAC remains up 29% YTD — the selloff reads as positioning, not distress, but the leverage gap between SPG and the rest of the segment keeps widening.

Read-through for Regency: Mall volatility is a reminder of why our cost of capital advantage persists — necessity-based cash flows compound quietly; discretionary formats re-rate violently in both directions.

Net Lease — Rate-Sensitive Drift Despite Clean Q2 Prints

Net lease slipped ~1% as the 10-year Treasury held near 4.30% and the Fed stayed on hold, with one cut still priced for Q4. Fundamentals were fine: Realty Income beat on revenue ($1.55B) with $1.09 Q2 AFFO and nudged full-year AFFO guidance to $4.44–$4.45, notably sourcing 73% of YTD equity from private capital rather than public issuance; NNN beat and raised to ~3.8% growth at the midpoint; Agree raised guidance on record Q2 investment volume. Essential (+5.7% YTD) remains the group's laggard as its middle-market tenant exposure gets extra scrutiny from the 2026 bankruptcy cycle.

Read-through for Regency: Realty Income's pivot to private capital is the sector's clearest signal that public equity remains too expensive to issue at an 8.7% median NAV discount — our self-funded development model and free cash flow sidestep that dilution math entirely.

WHAT CHANGED THIS WEEK

 

EARNINGS & GUIDANCE

  • Realty Income (O) — Q2 revenue of $1.55B beat by ~10%; AFFO of $1.09/sh with full-year AFFO guidance raised $0.02 at the midpoint to $4.44–$4.45. Fitch affirmed at A. Management's August deck emphasized a push into private capital vehicles targeting a "$15T addressable market."
  • NNN REIT (NNN) — Topped Q2 estimates and raised guidance, implying ~3.8% core FFO growth at the midpoint; portfolio of 3,711 properties carries a 10.1-year weighted average lease term.
  • Urban Edge (UE) — Record FFO-as-adjusted up 10% year-over-year; Q2 AFFO of $0.40 beat consensus and full-year guidance was raised to $1.50–$1.54.
  • Acadia (AKR) — Q2 FFO of $0.31 beat the $0.30 consensus on record leasing spreads and strong street-retail demand; full-year FFO growth guidance raised to 10%.

CAPITAL MARKETS

  • Sector issuance mix — REITs raised $20B in Q2 offerings per Nareit ($10.5B debt, $6.3B common equity); with most sectors closing Q2 at a median 8.7% discount to NAV, debt and ATM programs continue to dominate over marketed equity.
  • Private capital rotation — Realty Income disclosed 73% of 2026 YTD equity has come from private sources ($1.2B private capital, $1.0B insurance) versus just $0.8B public — a template other large caps are watching while public currencies screen expensive to issue.
  • Rates — Fed held at 3.75–4.00% with one cut still priced for Q4 2026; the 10-year Treasury sits near 4.30%, about 25 bps below its February peak, keeping fixed-rate CRE quotes in the high-5s.

TRANSACTIONS

  • Grocery-anchored volume at records — Roughly $12.8B of grocery-anchored investment traded in the four quarters through Q1 2026, with the institutional buyer share at a decade high; 85% of institutional retail investors now rank the format their top preference. Best-in-class centers are clearing at 5.25–5.5% caps.
  • Town Lane / Pantheon portfolio — Town Lane, alongside an international institutional partner, acquired ShopOne Centers and a 27-asset grocery-anchored portfolio from Pantheon — another sizeable private entry into the format.
  • SITE Centers (SITC) dispositions — The wind-down continued with sales including The Pike Outlets and Meadowmont Market; trailing revenue is down ~60% year-over-year as the remaining pool shrinks toward final distributions.

TENANT CREDIT

  • Watch list — RapidRatings flags Children's Place, QVC, and Walgreens as elevated-risk credits; Walgreens remains mid-program on its 1,200-store closure plan through 2027, a headwind for drugstore-adjacent inline demand but a shadow-supply source of well-located boxes.
  • 2026 filings running company-specific, not systemic — Nine retail bankruptcies and 14+ closure programs tracked through mid-year (Saks Global, QVC Group, Foot Locker closures; GameStop shut 470+ stores in January); casual dining is bifurcating, with FAT Brands in Chapter 11 while Texas Roadhouse and Chili's outperform.
  • Grocery consolidation — Kroger's $1.65B Giant Eagle transaction keeps grocery M&A in focus; anchor consolidation is a re-tenanting watch item but has so far supported, not weakened, credit quality at surviving banners.

Every private trade at those levels marks grocery-anchored NAVs higher and validates public portfolios still implied near 6.0% — a direct tailwind to Regency's asset value.

Surviving banners emerge with stronger credit and bigger store investment budgets — positive for renewals — but overlapping-banner markets face selective store rationalization landlords must underwrite center by center.

— NEUTRAL

Forecasters still see 25–50 bps of cap rate compression by year-end if long yields ease — upside optionality for stabilized grocery values, but no near-term relief on refinancing or acquisition math.

REGENCY PORTFOLIO READ-THROUGH

 

SUN BELT & MIDWEST MARKETS

The regional data continues to bifurcate in ways that favor our footprint mix: Sun Belt metros are still posting 3–5% retail rent gains, but pockets of new supply are shaving rent growth at the margin, while a band of Midwest markets — including Chicago and Minneapolis — has quietly emerged as an outperformer on stable demand and near-zero new construction. InvenTrust's +27.4% YTD run is the market's endorsement of pure Sun Belt exposure, but the Q2 starts at The Berkeley at Durbin Park (Jacksonville, $55M, Whole Foods/TJ Maxx-anchored) show we can still find development returns in high-growth corridors of Atlanta, Dallas, and Florida that acquisitions can't match at today's 5.25–5.5% private caps.

GROCERY ANCHOR HEALTH

Anchor credit remains the sturdiest part of the stack. Kroger's $1.65B Giant Eagle acquisition keeps consolidation moving — for our Kroger-anchored centers that means a bigger, better-capitalized tenant, though overlapping-banner trade areas warrant store-level monitoring as integration plans firm up. Publix, Kroger, and Albertsons continue signing on their standard 20-year primary terms with extension options, and PECO's numbers (Kroger 5.2% of ABR, Publix 4.9%, 98.4% anchor occupancy) confirm anchor demand for well-located boxes remains effectively at full occupancy across the peer set. No new distress signals emerged among the major banners this week.

LEASING & SMALL SHOP TRENDS

The peer prints frame exactly where the remaining leasing upside sits: Kimco hit a record 92.9% small-shop occupancy, PECO a record 95.5% inline, and Brixmor built a record $71M signed-but-not-commenced pipeline. Spreads remain historically wide — 40.4% on Kimco's new leases, 33.7% at PECO — telling us shop-space pricing power has not yet peaked even at record occupancy. For our own book, the priority is converting the 240 bps leased-to-commenced gap: roughly 69% of our $41M SNO pipeline is slated to commence by year-end, which is the cleanest source of 2027 NOI growth we have and warrants tight fixturization and delivery management through Q4.

ACQUISITION & DEPLOYMENT

With premium grocery-anchored product clearing privately at 5.25–5.5% and institutional demand at a decade high (Town Lane's 27-asset portfolio purchase being the latest evidence), buying stabilized core is dilutive to our ~6% implied cap — the math keeps pointing to development and redevelopment, where the $680M in-process pipeline is earning a 9% blended return at nearly 80% pre-leased. The planned ~$400M of 2026 starts should be protected even if acquisition pricing stays rich; conversely, if long yields ease into year-end as forecast, the 25–50 bps of expected cap rate compression would mark the completed pipeline higher almost immediately. Selective one-off buys still pencil where seller circumstances (SITC-style wind-downs, fund-life expirations) create off-market pricing wider than 6%.
