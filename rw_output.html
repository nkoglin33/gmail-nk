This Week's Read
  • JP Morgan upgrades REG to Overweight, sets $83 price target. On September 24, JP Morgan raised its rating on Regency Centers from Neutral to Overweight with a new $83 price target, citing resilient leasing fundamentals, raised 2026 guidance, and an attractive entry point after the broader REIT de-rating. REG finished the week up +0.38%, outperforming the grocery-anchored segment average. Mizuho maintained a neutral stance with a trimmed $76 target on September 26, reflecting more cautious rate-timing assumptions. The divergence in conviction between banks reflects the real debate heading into Q3: how quickly will rate headwinds recede relative to fundamental outperformance?
  • Grocery-anchored names were the week's relative standouts — PECO, CURB, REG, BRX all positive. While net lease and mall names faced continued selling pressure, the four top-quintile performers by 5D return were all grocery-adjacent: PECO (+0.62%), CURB (+0.46%), REG (+0.38%), and BRX (+0.14%). The grocery-anchored segment averaged -0.50% for the week versus -1.57% for mall and -1.56% for net lease — a clear quality-flight dynamic in a rate-elevated environment. Necessity retail's visible cash flow visibility is providing a defensive premium that market-rate office-adjacent and higher-duration formats cannot match.
  • Grocery-anchored cap rates holding at 6.0–6.8% despite rate headwinds. Industry data show grocery-anchored and open-air neighborhood center cap rates have remained range-bound at 6.0–6.8% across major markets, reflecting persistent investor demand for necessity retail with strong foot traffic and near-historic low vacancies. New retail construction is tracking 37% below 2025 supply, tightening the supply/demand equation further. This stability contrasts sharply with the pressure visible in net lease valuations, where rising rates directly compress the spread to long-term Treasuries.
  • Q3 2026 reporting season begins in approximately four weeks. Q3 ends September 30, and all nine of Regency's direct peers are expected to report October 21 – November 4. The next quarter's releases will be the first full read on whether the record Q2 occupancies across the peer set (KIM 96.4%, PECO 97.3%, REG 96.9%) have been sustained, and whether blended rent spread momentum — running 10–21% across the group in Q2 — has moderated under the pressure of a higher-rate leasing environment.
  • CBL pulls back -4.47% on the week but still leads the universe YTD at +40.30%. CBL & Associates gave back some of its outsized YTD run this week, falling -4.47% as profit-taking and rate sensitivity weighed on higher-beta mall names. Even after the pullback, CBL remains the top YTD performer across all 19 names at +40.30% and +73.26% on a trailing 1-year basis. The post-bankruptcy operational re-rating has driven significant multiple expansion, though the leverage profile and smaller portfolio scale remain points of distinction versus Simon or Macerich for institutional positioning.

Segment Cross-Section
Convenience / Strip
Curbline (CURB) finished the week of September 21–25 as the segment's sole name and a top-quintile 5D performer at +0.46%, bucking the broader REIT drift lower and affirming the convenience-retail premium in this rate environment. CURB's YTD gain stands at +22.62% — highest in the grocery-adjacent peer group — underpinned by its differentiated small-bay, high-traffic thesis: shorter lease terms create near-term repricing opportunities that are not correlated to long-duration rate sensitivity. Q2 results showed 96.5% occupancy and same-property NOI growth of roughly +3% excluding storm impacts. With Q3 reporting expected late October, investors will watch whether occupancy holds at these elevated levels and whether CURB's outperformance persists as the market re-prices rate risk.
Regency Read-Through: Curbline's positive 5D return in a broadly negative week validates the necessity-retail premium investors are attaching to supply-constrained, high-traffic formats — a dynamic that directly supports Regency's grocery-anchored positioning at the higher-density end of the same spectrum.
Grocery-Anchored / Power
The grocery-anchored peer group outperformed both the mall and net lease segments during the week of September 21–25, averaging -0.50% versus -1.57% for mall and -1.56% for net lease. PECO led the group at +0.62%, followed by REG (+0.38%) and BRX (+0.14%); these were three of the four top-quintile 5D performers across the full 19-name universe. KIM led the group on a YTD basis at +10.71%, reflecting record Q2 occupancy (96.4%) and embedded rent growth expectations. KRG was the week's notable laggard at -2.56%, trimming its YTD to +3.38% despite remaining the segment's top 1-year performer at +12.64%. The JP Morgan upgrade of REG to Overweight ($83 PT) was the most significant sell-side event of the week, broadly positive for the grocery-anchored narrative as Q3 approaches.
Regency Read-Through: The grocery-anchored segment's relative outperformance this week — with REG, PECO, and BRX all finishing positive — supports the JPMorgan upgrade thesis that REG's current price embeds an overly severe rate-risk discount for a company with near-record occupancy and 10%+ blended rent spreads.
Mall / Lifestyle
The mall/lifestyle segment averaged -1.57% for the week of September 21–25, weighed primarily by CBL (-4.47%) and SKT (-2.99%) as higher-beta names gave back recent gains. Simon (SPG, -0.24%) and Macerich (MAC, +0.04%) held up better, with MAC near flat as its ongoing balance-sheet improvement narrative attracted buyers. CBL's pullback is noteworthy context: despite the weekly decline, CBL remains the top YTD performer in the full 19-name universe at +40.30% and +73.26% trailing 1-year — the post-bankruptcy re-rating has run far and fast. AKR continues to lag year-to-date (-6.33% YTD, -4.18% 1-Yr), reflecting the overhang from its street-retail and urban-portfolio exposure, which carries more economic sensitivity than suburban formats.
Regency Read-Through: CBL's profit-taking pullback after an outsized YTD run illustrates the risks embedded in higher-beta mall re-rating stories; Regency's grocery-anchored model avoids discretionary-retail concentration risk while maintaining the open-air format advantage that distinguishes it from enclosed mall operators.
Net Lease
The net lease segment averaged -1.56% for the week of September 21–25, with GTY the worst performer at -3.31% and BNL down -1.51%. The mechanism remains rate sensitivity: net lease valuations are anchored to the spread between cap rates and long-term Treasuries, and the post-September FOMC environment continues to suppress that spread. EPRT is now negative across all three timeframes — -0.82% for the week, -10.55% YTD, -9.95% trailing 1-year — reflecting accumulated rate-repricing pressure since early 2026. NNN faces a $350M debt maturity in December 2026, with management guiding to 5.0–5.6% refinancing rates well above legacy coupons — a tangible cost headwind entering Q3 reporting. BNL's outperformance on a YTD basis (+8.87%) relative to EPRT and GTY continues to reflect portfolio diversification and lower single-tenant restaurant-credit concentration.
Regency Read-Through: Net lease's persistent rate sensitivity sharpens the contrast with Regency's leasing-spread-driven growth model — REG's 10%+ blended rent spreads represent an organic yield-growth mechanism that fixed-rate net lease structures cannot replicate, reducing duration risk and strengthening the relative fundamental case.
 
Regency Centers — Focus Issuer This Week
