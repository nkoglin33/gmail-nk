This Week's Read
  • Fed hikes 25bps to 3.75–4.00%; REIT sector sells off broadly. The September FOMC meeting delivered a 25bp rate increase, the latest in the current tightening cycle, removing near-term catalyst for the yield-compression relief that REIT valuations require. The broad FTSE NAREIT Composite fell roughly 2% on the week. Rate-sensitive net lease names bore the brunt, with GTY, EPRT, BNL, and NNN each falling 5–8% for the prior confirmed week. Regency's grocery-anchored peer set outperformed relative to net lease on the strength of necessity-based demand and near-record occupancies.
  • Grocery-anchored cap rates holding at 6.0–6.8% despite rate headwinds. Industry data show grocery-anchored and open-air neighborhood center cap rates have remained range-bound at 6.0–6.8% across major markets, reflecting persistent investor demand for necessity retail with strong foot traffic and near-historic low vacancies. New retail construction is tracking 37% below 2025 supply, tightening the supply/demand equation further. This stability contrasts with the pressure visible in net lease valuations, where rising rates directly compress the spread to long-term Treasuries.
  • JP Morgan upgrades REG to Overweight, sets $83 price target. On September 24, JP Morgan raised its rating on Regency Centers from Neutral to Overweight with a new $83 price target, citing the company's resilient leasing fundamentals, raised 2026 guidance, and what the firm described as an attractive entry point after the broader REIT sector de-rating. Mizuho maintained a neutral stance with a trimmed $76 target on September 26, reflecting divided conviction on the rate timing impact. Regency's updated investor presentation (September 11) reinforced the case for steady NOI growth and dividend durability.
  • Q3 2026 reporting season begins in approximately four weeks. Q3 ends September 30, and all nine of Regency's direct peers are expected to report October 21 – November 4. The next quarter's releases will be the first full read on whether the record Q2 occupancies across the peer set (KIM 96.4%, PECO 97.3%, REG 96.9%) have been sustained, and whether blended rent spread momentum — running 10–21% across the group in Q2 — has moderated under the pressure of a higher-rate leasing environment.
  • CBL continues its remarkable YTD run, now +46.9%. CBL & Associates has been the standout YTD performer in the mall/lifestyle segment and across the full 19-name universe, up 46.9% year-to-date (as of September 18 data) and +72% on a trailing 1-year basis. The post-bankruptcy operational and balance-sheet restructuring has driven significant re-rating, though CBL's leverage profile and smaller portfolio scale remain points of distinction versus Simon or Macerich for institutional positioning.

Segment Cross-Section
Convenience / Strip
Curbline (CURB) remains the sole name in this segment and has been a top-quintile YTD performer (+22.06%) underpinned by its differentiated convenience-retail thesis: small-bay, high-traffic sites where lease terms tend to be shorter and rent-roll turnover creates near-term repricing opportunities. The September FOMC rate hike pressured the stock (-3.38% for the prior confirmed week), but CURB's Q2 results showed 96.5% occupancy and same-property NOI growth of roughly +3% excluding storm impacts, confirming the fundamental health of the portfolio. With Q3 reporting expected late October, investors will watch whether occupancy holds at these elevated levels and whether the convenience premium in rent spreads is widening.
Regency Read-Through: Curbline's convenience-format performance validates the industry-wide thesis that necessity-anchored, supply-constrained retail is structurally insulated from e-commerce headwinds — a dynamic that directly supports Regency's grocery-anchored positioning at the higher-density end of that same spectrum.
Grocery-Anchored / Power
The grocery-anchored peer group held up better than the net lease sector in the rate-hike environment, with the segment averaging -2.89% for the prior confirmed week versus -6.63% for net lease — reflecting the embedded rent growth story that distinguishes mark-to-market leasing businesses from fixed-rate, long-duration net lease structures. KIM led the YTD cohort at +11.15% after reporting record 96.4% Q2 occupancy; KRG remained the top-quintile 1-year performer at +14.39%. At the lagging end, ADC (-5.53% YTD, -4.87% 1-year) and UE (-3.61% 1-year) reflect idiosyncratic headwinds — ADC's net-lease hybrid positioning makes it more rate-sensitive than the pure-play open-air peers, while UE's urban-edge assets carry some office-adjacency discount in portfolio perception. The sector-wide read entering Q3: lease spreads remain well above historical norms and grocery-anchored cap rates are holding at 6.0–6.8%, suggesting the valuation de-rating from rate pressure is disproportionate to underlying operating resilience.
Regency Read-Through: With JP Morgan's upgrade to Overweight ($83 PT) landing in the same week as the FOMC hike, the market is beginning to differentiate grocery-anchored quality from the broader REIT selloff — Regency's record occupancy and Q2 guidance raise position it as a likely beneficiary once rate clarity returns.
Mall / Lifestyle
The mall/lifestyle segment was the relative outperformer for the confirmed prior week at -0.89% average 5D return, partly because Simon (SPG, +0.24%) and Macerich (MAC, +0.09%) posted slight gains — a rare positive week for large-cap real estate amid the broader rate selloff. CBL's YTD run (+46.86%) remains the standout of the 19-name universe; the post-bankruptcy operational re-rating has significantly compressed the discount to NAV, though leverage and scale remain considerations for institutional positioning. Tanger (SKT) continues to benefit from outlet's perceived insulation from discretionary retail cycles, and Macerich's ongoing balance-sheet improvement narrative has pushed MAC up +27.49% on a trailing year basis. AKR remains the laggard in the segment (YTD -6.13%, 1-Yr -5.35%), reflecting the overhang from its street-retail and urban-portfolio exposure, which carries more economic sensitivity than suburban mall formats.
Regency Read-Through: The divergence within the mall segment — CBL and MAC outperforming while AKR and lifestyle names lag — reinforces the value of Regency's grocery-anchored model, which avoids discretionary-retail concentration risk while maintaining the open-air format advantage that distinguishes it from enclosed malls.
Net Lease
The net lease segment bore the sharpest rate-hike impact of any segment, falling an average of -6.63% for the prior confirmed week, with GTY (-8.15%), EPRT (-7.09%), and BNL (-5.97%) all posting material declines. The mechanism is direct: net lease valuations are anchored to the spread between cap rates and long-term Treasuries, and the September hike — combined with the market's hawkish read of the Fed's forward guidance — compressed that spread in real time. EPRT is now negative across all three timeframes (5D, YTD, 1-Yr) at -9.81% YTD and -11.39% trailing 1-year, reflecting accumulated rate-repricing pressure since early 2026. NNN faces a $350M debt maturity in December 2026, with management guiding to 5.0–5.6% refinancing rates well above legacy coupons — a tangible cost headwind entering Q3 reporting. BNL's YTD outperformance (+10.54%) relative to segment peers reflects its portfolio diversification and relatively lower exposure to single-tenant restaurant credits, which have faced more scrutiny in this rate environment.
Regency Read-Through: Net lease's rate sensitivity sharpens the contrast with Regency's leasing-spread-driven growth model — Regency's 10%+ blended rent spreads represent an organic yield-growth mechanism that net lease structures cannot replicate, reducing duration risk and strengthening the relative fundamental case as rates stay elevated.
 
Regency Centers — Focus Issuer This Week
Regency Centers pulled back modestly in the week ending September 18 (-2.24% on the confirmed close), consistent with the broader grocery-anchored peer group, as the market priced in the September Fed rate hike and the prospect of rates remaining elevated into year-end. Despite the rate headwind, REG’s fundamental positioning remains strong heading into Q3 reporting: Q2 showed 96.9% leased (record), same-property NOI growth of +3.8%, and raised 2026 FFO guidance to $4.84–$4.88. The JP Morgan upgrade to Overweight with an $83 price target on September 24 underscored the view that REG’s current price embeds an overly severe rate-risk discount for a company with near-record occupancy, a 54-year operating history, and sector-leading grocery-anchor tenancy coverage.
