Segment Cross-Section
Convenience / Strip
Curbline Properties (CURB) gained +2.1% through the week, heading into its August 3 earnings release at a +35% YTD level that reflects both fundamental momentum (Q1 same-property NOI +4.8%) and accelerating institutional demand for convenience retail formats. WSR's formal delisting on July 15 — Ares' $19.00/share acquisition closed — removes the last liquid strip comp and concentrates all public market price discovery in CURB alone. With ROIC, STOR, and now WSR absorbed by private capital, the necessity-retail strip format's public-market representative is now a single name, increasing CURB's benchmark importance for pricing and sentiment. Implied cap rates for institutional-quality convenience strip assets have compressed into the 5.5%–5.8% range nationally, consistent with CURB's trading level.
Regency Read-Through: The private-market absorption of WSR and the scarcity-value premium on CURB validate pricing power for well-located necessity-retail strip and grocery-anchored assets — Regency's format sits at the same cap-rate tier as CURB's best assets, reinforcing portfolio quality and NAV defensibility.
Grocery-Anchored
Regency Centers' Q2 beat drove the segment, with FFO of $1.21/share (vs. $1.20 consensus), same-property NOI growth of +3.8%, a near-97% lease rate, and cash rent spreads above 10% — all triggering target raises from Raymond James ($88, Outperform) and BTIG ($85, Buy). The modest step-down from Q1's +4.4% SS NOI reflects a tougher expense comparison that management guided to reverse in H2, with full-year SS NOI guidance raised by 40 bps. KIM's August 4 earnings are the segment's next catalyst — consensus expects $0.46 FFO (+4.6% YoY) after Stifel raised its target to $28 (Buy) and the stock has climbed 8.5% since its prior report. KRG (+0.8%) and IVT (+0.1%) drifted modestly positive as private-capital flows into grocery-anchored assets ($12.8B trailing-4Q volume) continued to provide a macro tailwind.
Regency Read-Through: REG's Q2 beat and guidance raise reinforce its role as the segment benchmark, with H2 SS NOI reacceleration and a development pipeline yielding above 9% blended returns providing multiple vectors of outperformance relative to peers entering the back half of 2026.
Power / Diversified
Agree Realty (ADC) gained +1.0% following its Q2 report showing $502M in investments at approximately 7.1% initial yields, with full-year guidance raised reflecting continued 11.3-year average lease-term deployment pace. ADC's 2026 acquisition program of $1.4–$1.6B at sub-5% leverage ratios positions the company as the segment's only operative benchmark — UBA was absorbed into Regency (August 2023) and PREIT trades illiquidly on the OTCQB (as "PRET") after its second post-bankruptcy restructuring. ADC's net-lease-adjacent model diverges from traditional power center ownership but provides the closest investable read-through on single-tenant convenience retail pricing and tenant credit health in the segment. Implied caps for ADC acquisitions (7.1%) remain ~150 bps wide to grocery-anchored private-market cap rates, compensating for single-tenant concentration risk.
Regency Read-Through: ADC's 7.1% acquisition yields versus Regency's sub-5.75% implied cap confirm the significant quality premium embedded in grocery-anchored multi-tenant open-air retail; Regency's development and redevelopment returns above 9% represent an even wider spread versus the current acquisition market than ADC can generate from acquisitions alone.
Mall / Lifestyle
Acadia Realty (AKR) led the segment with +1.3% after reporting Q2 results on July 28 — record leasing of $8.9M and signed-not-opened rent up 60% to $16.5M (80% from street/urban assets), alongside 11% YoY earnings growth confirming the urban street-retail recovery thesis. CBL delivered +1.2% on continued post-bankruptcy momentum (Q1 FFO of $1.73, raised dividend to $0.625/quarter, full-year FFO guidance $7.06–$7.19). SKT (+0.9%) and MAC (+0.5%) drift positively into their August 4 earnings: SKT raised its dividend 6.8% to $1.25 annualized (Q1 beat $0.59 vs. $0.57 estimate), while MAC carries the week's widest-range setup at $15.94–$26.68 over 52 weeks. SPG ($229.78) remains range-bound ahead of August 10, with Q1 domestic NOI growth of +6.7% and mall occupancy at 99.2% providing a strong fundamental base.
Regency Read-Through: Mall-sector NOI growth (+6.7% at SPG) reflects pent-up demand for experiential retail, but Regency's grocery-anchored tenants face structurally lower occupancy cost risk and dramatically less department-store/fashion e-commerce exposure — making REG's NOI quality more durable even at a lower headline growth rate.
Net Lease
The net lease segment produced the week's most volatile dispersion: Broadstone Net Lease (BNL) fell ~3% to $21.49 despite a Q2 beat (AFFO +2.6% to $0.39/share, full-year guidance raised to $1.55–$1.57), a textbook sell-the-news reaction that suggests elevated expectations heading into the print. Getty Realty (GTY) outperformed sharply — hitting a 52-week high of $34.93 after raising 2026 AFFO guidance for the second time to $2.52–$2.54 with Q2 acquisitions at 7.4% initial cash yields; KeyBanc raised its target to $36 (Overweight). EPRT (+1.5%) gained ground following its July Q2 print (revenue +18.1% to $161.9M, FFO $116.9M) as deployment momentum builds toward $1.2–$1.5B in 2026 investments. NNN's +17.7% YTD lead extends after its 37th consecutive annual dividend increase ($0.62/quarter), though sell-side consensus flags the stock as "fully priced" near-term.
Regency Read-Through: Net lease acquisition cap rates of 5.8–7.4% versus grocery-anchored private-market pricing of ~5.5–6.5% confirm institutional capital's preference for necessity-anchored multi-tenant formats — a validation of Regency's asset quality and the scarcity of its institutional-grade open-air grocery-anchored portfolio.
Regency Centers — Focus Issuer This Week
Regency Centers (REG) posted its Q2 2026 earnings during the week, delivering FFO of $1.21/share above the $1.20 consensus and revenue of $413.5M versus $411.1M expected, with same-property NOI growth of +3.8%, a near-97% lease rate, cash rent spreads above 10%, GAAP spreads approaching 20%, and 84% tenant retention — prompting management to raise full-year SS NOI guidance by 40 bps at the midpoint and guide core operating EPS growth above 5%. The Q2 SS NOI step-down from Q1's +4.4% pace was anticipated (tougher expense comparison), and management's guidance language signals a re-acceleration in H2, consistent with the company's near-cycle-high leased-to-occupied spread representing a substantial embedded NOI pipeline. Raymond James raised its price target to $88 (from $80, Outperform) and BTIG to $85 (from $79, Buy) in response to the beat, reflecting broad sell-side conviction that Regency's grocery-anchored, necessity-driven portfolio — with its institutional-quality Sun Belt and coastal infill footprint — remains the sector's most durable growth vehicle entering a backdrop of record institutional private-market demand for the format ($12.8B trailing-4Q transaction volume) and a still-wide ~145 bps public-private cap rate spread that supports premium REIT valuations for best-in-class operators.
