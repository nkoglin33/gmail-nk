Segment Cross-Section
Convenience / Strip
Curbline Properties (CURB) gave back 3.3% in the sector-wide selloff but holds a +27.6% year-to-date return — best of the open-air names (only CBL's +52.8% is higher universe-wide). The convenience strip format continues to benefit from near-zero new supply in infill suburban corridors, sustained tenant demand for sub-15,000 sf spaces from grocers, service-oriented tenants, and quick-service restaurants, and CURB's near-zero leverage (net debt/EBITDA of 0.5×) that gives it an acquisition-velocity advantage unavailable to higher-levered peers. CURB's Q2 record investment volume of $375M confirms institutional buyers are pricing convenience assets at sub-6% cap rates with no visible supply-side pressure.
Regency Read-Through: CURB's premium multiple and sub-6% private-market clearing prices underscore the cap-rate compression case for grocery-anchored centers broadly; Regency's ability to create comparable exposure through development at 9% blended yields remains the most compelling deployment alternative while acquisitions are priced at 5.25–5.5%.
Grocery-Anchored / Power
The 9-name composite fell 4.4% (simple average) in a broad risk-off week, even as the sector closed out a strong earnings run — the last of the direct REG peers (Urban Edge, Aug 6) reported, completing a unanimous Q2 beat-and-raise cycle across the comp group. No name was spared: ADC held in best at -2.8%, REG fell 3.9%, and KRG (-6.5%) and IVT (-6.2%) lagged as the whole comp group gave back post-earnings gains. The fundamental backdrop remains the strongest in the segment's recent history: record occupancy at multiple names, blended spreads averaging mid-teens, and SP NOI growth clustering between 3.5% and 5.8% across the nine peers. Private capital continues to validate public valuations, with institutional grocery-anchored transaction volume on a record pace at ~$12.8B trailing.
Regency Read-Through: With all 9 direct peers now reported and every one raising guidance, the Q2 earnings cycle eliminates any sector-specific execution concern for Regency; the focus now shifts entirely to REG's own signed-not-open pipeline (69% expected to commence by year-end) as the catalyst for 2027 NOI step-up.
Mall / Lifestyle
The 5-name mall/lifestyle composite was the week's clear laggard, with Macerich's 5.6% selloff on a soft Q2 print (SP NOI +1.9%, leverage 7.9× net debt/EBITDA) dragging the segment sharply negative. Tanger fell 3.3% in sympathy, while Simon was the segment's most defensive name (-2.8%) supported by its A-rated balance sheet and the expectation of its Q2 report today (August 10). Acadia was the universe's weakest name this week (-8.1%) despite posting the sector's best Q2 leasing metrics (street-retail SP NOI +8.7%; raised guidance to $1.24–$1.26 full-year FFO). CBL and SKT both reported Q2 results during the week; both showed stable occupancy and modest SP NOI growth but their outlet and value-oriented formats continued to lag grocery-anchored peers in spread momentum.
Regency Read-Through: Mall volatility — and the MAC/SKT selloff on perfectly acceptable underlying results — reinforces the durability premium investors assign to grocery-anchored necessity-based cash flows; the wider the mall/grocery spread gap grows, the clearer the relative value thesis for REG's open-air portfolio becomes.
Net Lease
The 4-name net lease composite drifted lower on the week as the 10-year Treasury held near 4.30% and the Fed remained on hold — reinforcing the rate-sensitivity that is net lease's dominant external variable. NNN REIT (Aug 5) beat Q2 estimates and raised full-year guidance to imply ~3.8% core FFO growth on a 3,711-property portfolio with 10.1-year weighted average lease term. Agree Realty (ADC) slipped -2.8% despite its record Q2 investment volume ($502M), and EPRT eased -2.1% as middle-market tenant scrutiny from the 2026 bankruptcy cycle (nine retail filings tracked YTD) weighs on sentiment. BNL and GTY each eased about -2.2%. Realty Income (O, not in our universe) disclosed a significant pivot toward private capital for equity sourcing — a template the sector watches closely.
Regency Read-Through: Net lease rate-sensitivity reinforces the structural advantage of Regency's multi-tenant, necessity-anchored lease structure — shorter lease terms and frequent mark-to-market opportunities provide built-in inflation protection that triple-net long-term leases do not, supporting a lower cap rate and higher implied multiple for grocery-anchored open-air.
 
Regency Centers — Focus Issuer This Week
Regency shares fell 3.9% in the sector-wide pullback even as the Q2 earnings cycle closed with all 9 direct competitors reporting — and every one raising 2026 guidance. REG's own Q2 print (reported July 29) remains the segment benchmark: Nareit FFO of $1.21/share (beat by $0.02), same-property NOI growth of +3.8%, portfolio 96.9% leased (+40 bps YoY), blended cash rent spread 10.4% (GAAP 19.5%), and full-year guidance raised for the second time to $4.84–$4.88 FFO / 3.7%–4.1% SP NOI growth. The 240 bps leased-to-commenced gap and a $41M SNO pipeline (69% expected to commence by year-end) remain the primary 2027 NOI driver that peer comparisons do not yet capture. Raymond James ($88, Outperform) and BTIG ($85, Buy) raised price targets following Q2.

Quarterly Synopsis — Full Content Mode
All 9 direct REG peers have now reported Q2 2026 (Urban Edge, the final peer, reported August 6, 2026). This synopsis reflects a full Q2 2026 cross-peer comparison.
I. Q2 2026 Earnings Cycle — Unanimous Beat-and-Raise. All 9 direct REG peers reported Q2 2026 results between July 24 and August 6, and every single one revised full-year guidance upward. REG beat consensus by $0.02 (Nareit FFO $1.21/share) and raised guidance for the second time to $4.84–$4.88 FFO / 3.7%–4.1% SP NOI. The broadest peer data point: not one direct competitor missed estimates, and every guidance revision was upward — a synchronized result that eliminates any sector-specific execution concern and frames H2 2026 as a clean translation of existing leasing momentum into NOI.
II. Occupancy & Leasing Scorecard. Portfolio leased rates across the 9-name comp set reached a cycle high: PECO 97.3% (record), REG 96.9% (+40 bps YoY), KIM 96.4% (record), CURB 96.5% (+20 bps), FRT ~95.5%, BRX and KRG in the mid-95s, UE ~95.1% (Q2 figures pending full supplemental), AKR N/D by traditional leased-rate metric. The defining theme: small-shop occupancy hit records at KIM (92.9%) and PECO (95.5% inline), confirming that the demand-supply imbalance for junior anchor and shop space has not yet relaxed even at peak occupancy. REG's 240 bps leased-to-commenced gap (~96.9% leased vs. ~94.5% paying) is the single largest source of identified future NOI among the comp set.
III. Rent Spread Scoreboard — Q2 2026 Full Peer Ranking. Renewal cash spreads, where disclosed (ranked): #1 PECO +21.2% (Q2, renewal only); #2 BRX ~19% (blended); #3 KRG +15.9% (blended); #4 FRT +15.0% (small-shop cash); #5 KIM +13.1% (blended pro-rata); #6 IVT +7.9% (renewal, confirmed Q2). Contractual escalators: KRG discloses a clean 2.0% contractual step; PECO provides escalator schedules in its supplemental; no other peer discloses a portfolio-wide contractual annual increase figure.
IV. Same-Property NOI Trends. Q2 2026 SP NOI growth across the comp set: BRX +5.8% (leader), AKR +8.7% street-retail / +5.1% overall (leader excluding format-specific), REG +3.8%, KIM +3.5%, KRG +3.7%, IVT +4.1%, CURB -0.5% (+3.1% excluding storm damage), PECO +3.8%, UE Q2 not yet confirmed. Every peer that raised guidance revised SP NOI growth upward. The underlying driver across the group: embedded rent steps on existing leases (most comp sets average 2%–3% annually), new-lease mark-to-market on rollovers at double-digit spreads, and the commencement of signed-not-open pipeline. BRX's $71M signed-but-not-commenced ABR pipeline and REG's $41M SNO pipeline are the two largest identified 2027 NOI pools in the comp set.
V. FFO & Guidance Trajectory — All 9 Raised. Full-year FFO / AFFO guidance revisions (Q2 2026, upward at every peer): REG $4.84–$4.88 (second raise of 2026); BRX $2.35–$2.37; FRT N/D full-year but +7% FFO YoY in Q2; KIM $1.81–$1.84; KRG N/D full-year; PECO N/D full-year; CURB $1.24–$1.26 OFFO; AKR $1.24–$1.26 (raised to guide above mid-point); ADC $4.57–$4.59 AFFO; UE $1.50–$1.54 AFFO (raised). The read on forward earnings quality: guidance midpoints have been revised upward in consecutive quarters at REG, BRX, AKR, and CURB — a pattern consistent with landlords undercommitting at the start of the year as SNO conversion timing becomes clearer mid-year.
VI. Sector Themes — Q2 2026. (a) Sub-1% new supply in grocery-anchored nationally continues to set the structural floor under rent growth. (b) Private-market transaction volume for grocery-anchored assets reached ~$12.8B trailing-twelve-months, with institutional buyers at a decade-high participation rate and clearing prices at 5.25–5.5% cap rates — ~55 bps inside the ~6.0% implied public cap for the grocery-anchored peer set, creating a private-market value bridge that supports further multiple expansion. (c) The 10-year Treasury held near 4.30% and the Fed remained on hold; one Q4 cut is priced, providing a potential tailwind for net-lease valuations and a modest cap-rate compression catalyst if realized. (d) Macerich's -6.8% selloff this week on soft SP NOI growth and 7.9× leverage illustrates the widening quality gap between necessity-anchored open-air (BRX 5.6× leverage, PECO 5.1×) and overleveraged discretionary-format operators — a gap that is pricing into the market in real time.
VII. Regency Positioning — Cross-Peer Context.
