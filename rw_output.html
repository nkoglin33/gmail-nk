This Week's Read
  • REG Q2 beat + raised guidance: grocery-anchored benchmark reaffirmed. Regency Centers reported Q2 2026 Nareit FFO of $1.21/share on July 29, beating consensus by $0.02; same-property NOI grew 3.8%, the portfolio ended 96.9% leased (+40 bps YoY), and blended cash rent spreads reached 10.4% (GAAP 19.5%). Management raised full-year Nareit FFO guidance to $4.84–$4.88 and SP NOI growth to 3.7%–4.1% — marking the second guidance raise of 2026. REG's grocery-anchored positioning, combined with sub-1% new supply across the format nationally, continues to give the company outsized pricing power heading into H2.
  • Earnings wave validates grocery-anchored across the peer set: BRX, FRT, KRG, PECO all raise guidance. Brixmor (BRX) delivered Q2 SP NOI of +5.8% with blended cash spreads of 19% and raised its full-year outlook to FFO $2.35–$2.37 / SP NOI 5.0%–5.75%. Federal Realty (FRT) reported FFO +7% YoY to $1.88 on record small-shop leasing (15% cash spread) and a 58th consecutive annual dividend increase to $1.16/quarter. Kite Realty (KRG) delivered SP NOI of +3.7% with leasing spreads of 15.9% and raised its SP NOI guidance to 3%–4%, while Phillips Edison (PECO) posted record leased occupancy of 97.3% with renewal spreads of 21.2%. The common thread: limited new supply, intense tenant demand, and escalating asking rents in grocery-anchored formats. All of these are direct read-throughs to Regency's competitive positioning.
  • AKR Q2: Street-retail recovery is structural, not cyclical. Acadia Realty Trust reported Q2 FFO of $0.31/share on July 28, with same-property NOI growth of 8.7% — 15.6% in street retail — and a company-record $8.9M of new annual base rent signed. The signed-but-not-yet-commenced pipeline of ~$16.5M in rent suggests sustained multi-quarter NOI step-ups through 2027. AKR raised its full-year FFO outlook to $1.24–$1.26 and expects its internal forecast to trend above the guidance midpoint. Its June 2026 equity offering at $21.95/share ($197.5M gross proceeds) provides dry powder for additional institutional-quality urban acquisitions.
  • Net lease splits: ADC sets a record, BNL sheds gains despite beat. Agree Realty (ADC) reported Q2 AFFO of $1.14/share (+7.4% YoY) on a company-record $502M of investments across 102 properties (7.0% cap rate, 11.2-year lease term), raised full-year AFFO guidance to $4.57–$4.59, and increased its monthly dividend to $0.267/share (+4.3% YoY). Broadstone Net Lease (BNL) beat FFO estimates ($0.39 vs. $0.38 consensus) and raised guidance to $1.55–$1.57, but the stock sold off on execution risk concerns around a $303M Colorado build-to-suit JV with a Fortune 20 tenant. GTY reported AFFO +5.1% to $0.62, its second guidance raise of 2026 (now $2.52–$2.54), while EPRT delivered AFFO of $0.50 (+18% YoY) on $300M invested at 7.8% yield.
  • August earnings calendar: KIM, SKT, MAC, NNN, SPG, UE, CBL all report this week or next. The Q2 earnings wave continues through the first two weeks of August. Kimco (KIM, reports Aug 4) is the largest grocery-anchored peer still outstanding; analysts expect FFO of ~$0.47/share. Tanger (SKT, Aug 4) and Macerich (MAC, Aug 4) present the mall-segment read. NNN REIT (Aug 5) and Simon Property Group (~Aug 10) follow. Urban Edge (UE, Aug 6) and CBL Properties (Aug 6) round out the current quarter's retail calendar. Results will determine whether the strong grocery-anchored momentum seen this week extends to the second tier of operators and whether mall REITs sustain the occupancy gains begun in Q1.

Segment Cross-Section
Convenience / Strip
Curbline Properties (CURB) continued its strong 2026 run, adding +2.3% on the week to lead all segments with +25.1% year-to-date. The format benefits from sustained consumer preference for drive-to convenience retail and near-zero new supply in infill suburban corridors. Operator fundamentals across the convenience/strip sector remain among the healthiest in listed retail, driven by grocer and service-tenant demand for sub-15,000-sf spaces.
Regency read-through: CURB's outperformance underscores investor appetite for formats with grocery-adjacent positioning and high daily-needs traffic — a dynamic that also supports Regency's open-air, grocery-anchored portfolio's relative premium.
Grocery-Anchored / Power
The 9-name grocery-anchored/power composite gained +1.2% for the week, with BRX, FRT, KRG, and PECO all reporting Q2 beats and guidance raises that confirmed the segment's operational momentum. Blended cash rent spreads across reporting peers averaged mid-teens, occupancy held above 96% for the group, and same-property NOI growth guidance for 2026 was revised upward at every peer. Sub-1% new supply nationally continues to provide structural pricing power for well-positioned open-air centers.
Regency read-through: REG's Q2 beat — 96.9% leased, 10.4% blended cash spread, second guidance raise of 2026 — validates its grocery-anchored positioning; the peer results eliminate any sector-specific execution concern and shift focus squarely to Regency's own NOI step-up pipeline into H2.
Mall / Lifestyle
The 5-name mall/lifestyle composite rose +1.4% for the week, narrowly leading grocery-anchored in absolute terms, though the group's operating characteristics remain more varied. AKR's Q2 street-retail results (SP NOI +8.7%, record new-lease signings of $8.9M) supported the lifestyle sub-segment, while CBL's +21.5% YTD gain reflects recovery from a low base. SPG's Q2 report, expected around August 10, is the segment's defining catalyst; consensus expects steady Simon Prime occupancy at or above 95.5%.
Regency read-through: Mall-format performance remains largely independent of Regency's open-air thesis, though AKR's urban street-retail momentum is directionally consistent with the broader theme that necessity-anchored, convenience-oriented physical retail continues to outperform expectations.
Net Lease
The 4-name net lease composite added +0.2% on the week, lagging the other three segments as BNL's post-earnings selloff (-2.0%, execution risk concerns on a $303M Colorado build-to-suit JV) offset record investment volume from ADC and solid AFFO beats from GTY and EPRT. ADC's Q2 — $502M deployed at 7.0% cap rate, 11.2-year lease term — set a company record and illustrated the sector's defensive cash-flow profile. Net lease valuations remain rate-sensitive, with the 10-year Treasury's trajectory the dominant external variable heading into August.
Regency read-through: Net lease sector results have limited direct read-through to Regency's multi-tenant open-air model, but ADC's sustained high-volume investment deployment at 7%+ cap rates provides a useful cross-sector valuation benchmark for well-located retail real estate.
Regency Centers — Focus Issuer This Week
Regency Centers (REG) reported Q2 2026 results on July 29, delivering Nareit FFO of $1.21/share vs. $1.19 consensus, same-property NOI growth of +3.8%, a portfolio-wide leased rate of 96.9% (+40 bps YoY), and blended cash rent spreads of 10.4% (GAAP 19.5%) — marking the second guidance raise of 2026, with full-year Nareit FFO now guided to $4.84–$4.88 and SP NOI growth to 3.7%–4.1%. The Q2 pace moderated slightly from Q1's +4.4% SP NOI (anticipated given a harder expense comparison), and management's guidance language signals re-acceleration in H2 as the signed-but-not-yet-open pipeline — representing a wide leased-to-occupied spread near cycle highs — begins to convert. The result was met positively by the sell side, with price target increases from Raymond James ($88, Outperform) and BTIG ($85, Buy), and reinforces REG's positioning as the grocery-anchored benchmark heading into an August earnings slate that includes KIM (Aug 4) and UE (Aug 6) — the two remaining direct peers that have not yet reported Q2.
