WEEK ENDING July 17, 2026

Public Retail REIT Universe

21 issuers · 5 segments · ~$285B aggregate market cap
This Week's Read
  • Mid-July Rally Accelerates — Net Lease and Malls Lead the Universe. Retail REITs posted their strongest broad rally in months the week of July 14–18, with net lease (+3.6% avg) and Mall/Lifestyle (+3.7% avg) topping all segments. EPRT (+5.1%), CBL (+6.0%), ADC (+4.0%), and KRG (+4.0%) all hit 52-week highs or multi-year highs on the week. For Regency, the sector-wide re-rating reflects sustained investor rotation into income-generating open-air assets as Q2 earnings season approaches — a favorable setup for REG's July 29 report.
  • REG Breaks to New 52-Week High, Pre-Earnings Conviction Building. Regency Centers (REG) gained approximately +3.0% on the week, reaching a new 52-week high of $81.93 and closing near $82.36 — outpacing the grocery-anchored segment average of +2.8%. Analysts project $1.20 FFO/share for Q2 (July 29 release), a +3.5% YoY increase, with full-year same-property NOI guidance of +3.25%–3.75% well-supported by Q1's +4.4% result and 96.6% portfolio occupancy. The consensus fair value target of ~$84.63, with 9 of 21 analysts at Strong Buy, suggests the stock still has room to run even after a +14.4% YTD gain.
  • Whitestone REIT Acquired — Public Grocery-Anchored Peer Set Shrinks Again. The $1.7B all-cash acquisition of Whitestone REIT (WSR) by Ares Real Estate funds closed approximately July 14 at $19.00/share, formally removing the Sun Belt-focused convenience REIT from the investable universe. Combined with Blackstone's ROIC take-private (February 2025) and GIC/Oak Street's STORE Capital acquisition (February 2023), the public retail REIT universe has lost three names to institutional buyers in three years — validating private cap rates and concentrating institutional flows into high-quality survivors like REG, KIM, and KRG.
  • Net Lease Surges on Earnings Previews and Dividend Momentum. EPRT led all 21 issuers with a +5.1% gain to $32.92 ahead of its July 22–23 Q2 report, following a Q1 EPS beat and a recent dividend increase to $0.32/quarter (+3% QoQ). NNN REIT climbed +3.8% to $49.22 on its 37th consecutive annual dividend increase ($0.62/quarter, +3.3%) — a quality signal that drew income-oriented buyers. BNL hit a new 52-week high of $22.15 as BMO raised its price target to $26 (from $24). Net lease implied cap rates of 5.5–6.5% continue to highlight the relative value of Regency's multi-tenant grocery-anchored model, which generates 3–4× higher SS NOI growth.
  • Grocery-Anchored Cap Rate Compression Accelerates — Private Market Catching Up. Private-market grocery-anchored transaction volume hit $11B in 2025 (+42% YoY), with institutional share at 27% — the highest in over a decade. Best-in-class assets now trade at 5.5% cap rates in the private market, while the public-private REIT cap rate spread — 145–169 bps over the 10-year Treasury vs. private appraisals at only ~24 bps over — has persisted for a record 17 quarters. This compression directly supports NAV expansion for Regency's top-tier Publix- and Kroger-anchored portfolio, where private buyers are actively competing with public REIT acquisition pipelines on nearly identical assets.

Segment Cross-Section
Convenience / Strip
Curbline Properties (CURB) gained approximately +1.5% on the week as institutional buyers digested the company's July 9 follow-on equity offering of 10 million shares at $30.85/share, raising $308.5 million for future acquisitions; the stock held near $30.90 with YTD performance remaining exceptional at +31.9%, confirming strong investor conviction in CURB's convenience-strip growth story. Whitestone REIT (WSR) was formally de-listed following the closing of its $1.7B Ares Real Estate acquisition at $19.00/share around July 14 — the third publicly traded grocery-anchored/convenience REIT absorbed by private capital since early 2023. Non-traded Inland Real Estate Income Trust (INRE) continues without public price discovery.
Regency Read-Through: The WSR closing concentrates the investable convenience/strip universe to a single significant public name (CURB), further directing open-air institutional capital toward larger, better-diversified peers like Regency — and validating sub-6% cap rate deal pricing that aligns with REG's acquisition underwriting.
Grocery-Anchored
Grocery-anchored REITs were the week's second-best segment (+2.8% avg), led by Kite Realty (KRG, +4.0%), which hit a new 52-week high of $29.40 on July 16, driving its 1-year return to +34.55% ahead of Q2 earnings on July 30; REG gained +3.0% to a new 52-week high of $81.93 on broad pre-earnings conviction (Q2 report July 29), while InvenTrust (IVT, +2.1%) consolidated near its all-time high of $36.37. Kimco (KIM, +2.0%, YTD +25.6%) held near its 12-month range top of $25.47 on the continued momentum of its $77M "signed-not-opened" pipeline and 96.3% occupancy. ROIC remains locked at Blackstone's $17.50 take-private price, contributing no public price discovery. Private-market grocery-anchored cap rates continue compressing toward 5.5% for top-tier assets, narrowing a historically wide public-private spread.
Regency Read-Through: With REG breaking to a new 52-week high and peer KRG outperforming on the week, the grocery-anchored segment is signaling broad fundamental strength ahead of Q2 earnings — REG's reported +4.4% SS NOI and 96.6% occupancy remain the sector-leading combination that analysts expect to sustain through the second half.
Power / Diversified
Agree Realty (ADC) surged +4.0% on the week to $80.75, its strongest single-week gain of 2026, driven by pre-earnings positioning ahead of Q2 results (July 30–31), robust analyst consensus with a blended price target near $84, and investor focus on its July 31 ex-dividend date for the $0.267/month payout — reflecting 99%+ investment-grade tenancy. The segment's implied cap rate of 4.80% highlights ADC's premium valuation among open-air/net-lease peers. Urstadt Biddle (UBA) was fully absorbed into Regency Centers in the 2023 all-stock merger and no longer trades; PREIT (OTC: PRET) remains effectively inactive at a $2.7M market cap on OTCQB, with negative shareholder equity and no institutional coverage.
Regency Read-Through: ADC's 4.80% implied cap rate and +4.0% weekly gain underscore the market's premium pricing for high-quality, necessity-anchored income — a valuation tailwind for REG as investors continue to narrow the gap between net-lease and multi-tenant grocery-anchored implied cap rates.
Mall / Lifestyle
Mall/Lifestyle was the week's top-performing segment alongside Net Lease (+3.7% avg), fueled by a broad rotation into higher-yield, higher-beta retail names as rate expectations stabilized. CBL & Associates led all mall names with a +6.0% week, approaching its July 2 all-time high of $55.78 as recovered shopping center fundamentals translated into 117% one-year total returns; Macerich (MAC, +5.5%) hit a new 52-week high of $26.16 on July 18, extending its stunning YTD gain of +42% and 1-year return of +61% as investor sentiment on enclosed regional malls reached multi-year highs. Simon Property Group (SPG, +3.5%) hit a new 52-week high of $230.82 on July 17, with BofA, JPMorgan, Truist, Citi, and UBS all raising price targets in early July — reversing the prior week's Deutsche Bank downgrade hesitance. Tanger (SKT, +2.0%) hit $41.57, a new 52-week high, on dividend confirmation ($0.3125/quarter, ex-date July 31). Acadia (AKR, +1.5%) trailed the segment as equity offering overhang from its June 9M-share issuance at $21.95 lingered ahead of Q2 earnings on July 28.
Regency Read-Through: The mall segment's multi-year high valuations and compressed cap rates suggest limited further upside for enclosed formats — a contrast that supports Regency's open-air grocery-anchored model as the more sustainable long-term compounder, with superior SS NOI growth and lower leverage than most mall peers.
Net Lease
Net lease matched Mall/Lifestyle as the week's top-performing segment (+3.6% avg) on a combination of earnings previews and dividend catalysts. Essential Properties (EPRT, +5.1% to $32.92) was the week's #1 performer across all 21 issuers, surging ahead of its July 22–23 Q2 report on strong sector momentum and a recently raised quarterly dividend ($0.32/share, +3% QoQ); NNN REIT (+3.8% to $49.22) declared its 37th consecutive annual dividend increase to $0.62/quarter (+3.3%), drawing yield-focused buyers and extending its YTD gain to ~+10%. Broadstone Net Lease (BNL, +3.5%) hit a new 52-week high of $22.15 as BMO Capital raised its price target to $26 ("Outperform") following announcement of a $303M joint-venture investment in a Colorado advanced technology facility. Getty Realty (GTY, +2.0%) held near its 52-week high of $34.80 ahead of Q2 earnings on July 22, with 1-year total returns of +31.45% and a YTD gain of +28%. STORE Capital remains taken private with no public data.
Regency Read-Through: Net lease implied cap rates of 5.50–6.50% with minimal SS NOI growth (0–1%) versus REG's 5.50% implied cap rate with +4.4% SS NOI growth illustrates Regency's compelling growth-adjusted value: investors willing to pay up for net lease income are beginning to recognize REG offers equivalent safety with materially superior organic growth — a differentiator that should narrow the valuation gap through 2026 earnings season.
Regency Centers — Focus Issuer This Week
Regency Centers (REG) surged approximately +3.0% in the week ending July 18, reaching a new 52-week high of $81.93 and closing near $82.36, outperforming the grocery-anchored segment average of +2.8% and extending its YTD gain to +14.4%. Analyst conviction ahead of Q2 2026 earnings (report July 29, call July 30) has been building: with 9 of 21 covering analysts at Strong Buy and a consensus fair value estimate of ~$84.63 — representing ~3% upside from current levels — the sell-side expects Q2 FFO/share of $1.20 (+3.5% YoY) to be supported by same-property NOI momentum near or above the 3.25%–3.75% full-year guidance range and continued leasing execution on Regency's 96.6%-occupied portfolio. Competitively, the week's closure of Ares' $1.7B Whitestone acquisition (the second grocery-anchored peer removed from public markets in 15 months, following Blackstone's ROIC take-private) concentrates institutional capital into the remaining investable names, with REG benefiting disproportionately as the dominant, scaled, investment-grade pure-play grocery-anchored REIT — a structural positioning advantage that the public-private cap rate spread of 145–169 basis points over the 10-year Treasury continues to validate as private buyers compete directly with Regency for identical assets at or below its implied cap rates.
