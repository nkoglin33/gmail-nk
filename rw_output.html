  • Grocery-anchored supply gap widens. CBRE’s 2026 investor survey finds 85% of retail investors favor grocery-anchored centers — the most preferred retail format by a wide margin. New supply remains near-zero in most MSAs; Kroger, Publix and Walmart Neighborhood Market expansions continue to drive grocer-led leasing demand. Grocery-anchored transaction volume hit $11B in 2025 (JLL data, +42% YoY), reinforcing the format’s defensive positioning as the rate cycle peaks.

Segment Cross-Section
CONVENIENCE / STRIP
Curbline (CURB) returned ‑4.46% for the week ending October 9, the weakest performance in the convenience-strip segment this quarter and one of the broader market's laggards for the week. The selloff tracks macro rate headwinds rather than any fundamental deterioration — the company is approaching its Q3 reporting date (October 29, est.) having demonstrated defensible occupancy (96.5%) and SP NOI growth of +3.1% ex-storm impacts in Q2. CURB's +16.37% YTD return reflects strong year-to-date momentum; the single-week reversal is consistent with the broader risk-off tone that hit higher-beta convenience and proximity-retail names disproportionately. The Brixmor-Slate deal remains a reference data point for convenience-format valuations in Q3 analyst commentary.
Regency Read-Through: CURB’s outparcel and convenience-strip format competes directly with REG’s small-shop and shadow-anchor space; continued occupancy strength in this format supports REG’s same-property NOI growth assumption heading into Q3 despite the weekly price volatility.
GROCERY-ANCHORED / POWER
The segment averaged ‑0.74% for the week ending October 9, making it the best-performing segment on a relative basis in a broadly negative week. REG led the group at +0.63% — the only large-cap grocery-anchored name in positive territory — while BRX (‑0.33%) and KIM (‑0.36%) held up well. FRT and PECO each fell ‑0.50%; IVT (‑2.04%) and KRG (‑1.97%) lagged. The 10-year yield sustaining above 5% in early October is a macro headwind, but private-market conviction remains intact: the Brixmor-Slate deal prices grocery-anchored assets at implied cap rates near the 6.7% national benchmark. KIM leads segment YTD performance at +9.32% while ADC trails at ‑8.44%. Q3 earnings begin October 26–27 (BRX, PECO) with all operating metrics currently Q2 carryforwards.
Regency Read-Through: REG’s relative outperformance this week (+0.63% vs. segment avg ‑0.74%) and record leased occupancy (96.9%) with blended +10.4% cash spread and raised guidance provide a strong Q3 setup; the Brixmor-Slate cap-rate validation is directionally consistent with REG’s portfolio mark, though rate-driven multiple compression could cap near-term price appreciation.
MALL / LIFESTYLE
The Mall/Lifestyle segment fell ‑3.55% on average for the week ending October 9, the weakest segment on a 5-day basis. CBL led declines at ‑11.32% for the week — a sharp reversal after its strong YTD run (still +31.1% YTD) — as profit-taking accelerated on one of the market’s highest-beta recovery names. MAC fell ‑3.47% and AKR dropped ‑2.09%. SPG (‑0.76%) and SKT (‑0.09%) held up comparatively well as the 5-day laggards. The segment’s YTD average of +10.66% and 1-year average of +21.87% continue to reflect a multi-year recovery narrative, though profit-taking on high-momentum names like CBL may persist into Q3 earnings.
Regency Read-Through: Mall weekly underperformance driven largely by CBL profit-taking underscores the idiosyncratic risk in that segment’s YTD run; REG’s open-air grocery-anchored model does not face the same format-specific restructuring risk or high-momentum reversion dynamic that pressured mall stocks this week.
NET LEASE
Net lease averaged ‑1.65% for the week ending October 9, with no positive names in the group. A 10-year yield sustaining above 5% creates the most direct competitive pressure on long-duration net-lease cash flows, where cap-rate spreads to Treasuries compress most visibly. EPRT (‑2.05% weekly, ‑14.46% YTD) and NNN (‑1.73%) led declines; GTY (‑1.21%) held up best within the segment. The segment’s YTD average of ‑1.50% and 1-year average of ‑2.63% reflect persistent rate headwinds. GTY leads the group YTD at +1.79%; BNL reports October 28 and NNN November 4.
Regency Read-Through: Net-lease rate sensitivity underscores REG’s structural advantage — shorter effective lease terms, frequent re-leasing events, and grocery-anchor traffic stability reduce duration risk relative to triple-net single-tenant peers. REG’s FFO guidance does not embed the Treasury-spread vulnerability that has compressed net-lease multiples this cycle.
 
Regency Centers — Focus Issuer This Week
Regency Centers (REG) closed October 9, 2026 at $72.05 (+4.37% YTD), the sole large-cap grocery-anchored name in positive territory on the week (+0.63%), ahead of its Q3 2026 earnings call scheduled November 5. Following the strong Q2 print on July 29 — 96.9% portfolio leased (+40bps YoY), 10.4% blended cash rent spread, SP NOI growth of +3.8%, and raised full-year FFO guidance of $4.84–$4.88 — Wells Fargo carries an Overweight with an $88 price target and UBS stands at $81. The Brixmor-Slate Grocery transaction (Sep 28, ~$636M for 23 centers at ~96% leased, FL/GA/Carolinas) crystallizes private-market pricing in REG’s competitive tier: the implied cap rate near the ~6.7% national benchmark is directionally consistent with public-market valuations for REG’s premium grocery-anchored portfolio, though REG’s higher asset quality and major-market concentration should command a tighter cap-rate premium. The 10-year yield sustaining above 5% in early October is a macro headwind to watch heading into Q3 reporting, but REG’s rent-escalator and re-leasing optionality offer more rate resilience than the long-duration net-lease peers hit hardest this week.
