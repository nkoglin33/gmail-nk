This Week's Read
  • REIT universe broadly rallied into Q3 earnings season. With 16 of 19 tracked names posting positive 5-day returns, the week of Oct 13–16 saw the sector recover ground lost in August–September.   • Q3 2026 earnings season starts this week — 7 of 9 direct peers report Oct 27–29. BRX and PECO report Oct 27; UE and AKR on Oct 28; KIM, KRG, and CURB on Oct 29; FRT on Oct 30; REG last on Nov 5. The rapid clustering means the quarterly synopsis can be populated within two weeks. Key watch items: whether rent spread leadership (PECO at +21.2% renewal, Q2) holds, and if KIM and BRX can confirm positive occupancy trends.
  • Mall REITs continue strong 2026 run — CBL +70% trailing 1-year. CBL & Associates' post-restructuring appreciation (+33.7% YTD, +69.8% 1-Yr) reflects continued recovery in mall cash flows. MAC (+24.4% YTD) and SPG (+11.0% YTD) further confirm that premium and recovering malls are drawing capital. Analysts expect SPG Q3 EPS of ~$3.32, consistent with the earnings recovery narrative.
  • REG trading ~15% below consensus analyst target ahead of Q3. With shares at $73.24 and consensus targets clustering $83–$87 (Bank of America $87 Buy, Ladenburg Thalmann $85 Hold), the discount reflects sector rate uncertainty rather than fundamental weakness. Q2 beat ($0.61 vs. $0.59 consensus; $413.5M revenue vs. $411.1M estimated) and FY guidance of $4.84–$4.88 FFO per share is intact. Nov 5 report is the next catalyst.

Grocery-Anchored / Power
The segment averaged +1.61% for the week, with KIM leading (+3.38%) on continued positive sentiment around its post-RPT Realty integration and record 96.4% occupancy heading into Q3 results (Oct 29). The week's broad participation — 8 of 9 names positive — reflects market confidence ahead of an earnings cluster where every direct Regency peer reports Oct 27–30. ADC (–6.93% YTD, –10.07% 1-Yr) remains the segment outlier; its investment-grade net-lease-adjacent exposure to convenience retail has underperformed amid higher-for-longer rate pressure. Grocery-anchored cap rates at ~6.7% nationally and $11B in 2025 transaction volume (+42% YoY) signal the asset class continues to attract institutional demand.
Regency Read-Through: Positive peer tape and stable transaction cap rates support Regency's asset quality thesis; Nov 5 Q3 report should confirm whether its 96.9% leased rate and +3.8% SP NOI from Q2 held into Q3.
Mall / Lifestyle
Mall/Lifestyle was the week's strongest segment (+2.22% avg 5D), led by MAC (+3.19%) and SPG (+3.02%), both approaching or setting multi-year highs. CBL's post-restructuring recovery story remains striking (+33.7% YTD, +69.8% 1-Yr), driven by improving occupancy and rental rates in its mid-market portfolio. AKR (–9.49% YTD) is the segment laggard — its street-retail exposure in coastal markets faces structurally higher vacancy, though a Q3 earnings date of Oct 28 may provide a reset. SPG Q3 EPS consensus is ~$3.32 per share.
Regency Read-Through: Mall outperformance confirms broad retail real estate recovery is not sector-specific; Regency's grocery-anchored focus benefits from the same consumer-traffic tailwind without the department-store co-tenancy risk.
Net Lease
Net lease showed a split personality this week — NNN (+3.23%) and EPRT (+2.84%) led weekly gains, but both carry negative 1-Year returns, reflecting higher-for-longer rate sensitivity in the long-duration lease segment. EPRT remains the universe's worst-performing name YTD (–12.04%) and on a 1-Yr basis (–14.43%), with single-tenant exposure to experience-retail tenants under broader pressure. BNL (–0.71%) was the only name to decline this week. GTY (+3.69% YTD) outperforms within net lease on its petroleum/convenience focus. NNN confirmed Q3 reporting for November 4.
Regency Read-Through: Net lease rate sensitivity underscores why Regency's shorter-WALT, mark-to-market grocery-anchored portfolio continues to attract investor preference as a more durable retail REIT format.

Rent-step disclosure quality note
Contractual rent escalator (rent-step) disclosure is thin industry-wide among shopping center REITs. Among the 9-peer direct comp set, only KRG (~1.5–2.0% annually) and PECO consistently provide a clean contractual escalator figure in public filings. Most peers do not separately quantify step-up schedules. This is an industry-wide disclosure limitation, not a gap in this report’s research process.
