Convenience / Strip
Curbline Properties (CURB), the segment's sole member, continues to trade as a premium within the open-air universe — +27.6% YTD through the last confirmed close (Aug 7) reflects investor appetite for its pure-play convenience-format narrative following the 2024 spin from Site Centers. Q2 2026 results showed 96.5% leased (+20bps sequential), full-year OFFO guidance of $1.24–$1.26, and SP NOI growth of +3.1% excluding storm damage. With Q3 reporting expected in late October, the watch items are whether occupancy can push toward 97% and whether the storm-impacted NOI drag (roughly 360bps impact in Q2) reverses in H2.
Regency Read-Through: CURB's outperformance validates market demand for small-bay, convenience-oriented formats — the same tenant mix that anchors Regency's own small-shop leasing pipeline. Strong CURB results support higher renewal spread assumptions for comparable inline formats in REG's portfolio.
Grocery-Anchored / Power
The 9-ticker segment averaged -4.4% in the last confirmed trading week (ending Aug 7) but remains up an average of +14.2% YTD, grounded in exceptional fundamentals: PECO hit 97.3% leased (record), KIM hit 96.4% (record), and REG reached 96.9% (+40bps YoY). The Labor Day week (August 31 – September 4) features a shortened 4-day trading calendar; price data for this cycle is unavailable due to the ongoing API proxy block. Underlying Q2 operating metrics — uniform beat-and-raise across all 9 peers — give the segment strong footing into Q3 earnings season (expected late October). ADC lags the group YTD at +5.0%, reflecting its more conservative net-lease-adjacent format and lower-beta profile.
Regency Read-Through: REG's 3.8% SP NOI growth and $41M SNO pipeline position the company for continued NOI acceleration in H2 2026 as leases commence. The peer-wide beat-and-raise cycle removes any sector-specific execution overhang and creates a cleaner path to multiple re-rating.
Mall / Lifestyle
The Mall/Lifestyle segment delivered the strongest YTD performance of the four segments (+24.7% avg through Aug 7), led by CBL's post-emergence recovery story (+52.8% YTD) and Macerich's luxury-repositioning thesis (+32.2% YTD). SPG held steady near its all-time high following its Q2 beat (FFO +7.9%, domestic property NOI +8.5%, occupancy 96.0%), though Deutsche Bank downgraded to Hold ($220 target) in July citing full valuation. AKR remains the segment laggard at +0.5% YTD, reflecting its urban street-retail concentration and higher-beta occupancy profile. Q3 reports for Mall names are expected in late October through early November.
Regency Read-Through: Continued mall sector strength signals healthy consumer engagement broadly, though Regency's grocery-anchored open-air format is structurally differentiated from enclosed and premium-outlet formats. Mall traffic data in Q3 will provide a useful read on back-to-school and early holiday demand.
Net Lease
The Net Lease segment was the relative outperformer in the last confirmed trading period (avg -2.0% vs. -4.4% for grocery-anchored), reflecting its long-duration, contracted-income profile as a defensive posture. GTY (+22.0% YTD) and BNL (+20.5% YTD) have been the best performers in the group, with NNN slightly behind at +18.2%. EPRT continues to underperform at +3.3% YTD, weighed by acquisition-volume concerns and elevated leverage relative to NNN and BNL. The 10-year Treasury near 4.3% remains a modest cap-rate headwind for the group; a Q4 Fed cut (currently priced by markets) could provide a modest tailwind to net-lease valuations.
Regency Read-Through: Net lease's relatively defensive performance signals that markets continue to reward long-duration cash flow certainty. For Regency, the spread between grocery-anchored cap rates (~6.0% implied) and net-lease cap rates (~6.5%–7.0%) has compressed, which reinforces the relative valuation case for quality open-air assets.
