CONVENIENCE / STRIP
Curbline (CURB) returned ‑0.67% for the week ending October 2, lagging the mall segment but holding up better than the net-lease group. The single-issuer convenience-strip segment is approaching its Q3 reporting date (October 29, est.) having demonstrated defensible occupancy (96.5%) and SP NOI growth of +3.1% ex-storm impacts in Q2. The Brixmor-Slate deal will be a reference data point for convenience and proximity-retail valuations in Q3 analyst commentary, as the ~23 acquired Slate centers overlap somewhat with CURB's convenience-format positioning.
Regency Read-Through: CURB's outparcel and convenience-strip format competes directly with REG's small-shop and shadow-anchor space; continued occupancy strength in this format supports REG's same-property NOI growth assumption heading into Q3.
GROCERY-ANCHORED / POWER
The segment averaged ‑1.49% for the week (as of October 2 close), with PECO the sole gainer (+0.99%) on defensive grocery-only positioning. Rate headwinds from a 10-year yield surging above 5% in late September pressed the group broadly; KIM (‑0.89%) held up best among the larger names while FRT (‑3.47%) and REG (‑2.23%) underperformed. The Brixmor-Slate announcement prices similarly-leased grocery-anchored assets at implied cap rates near the 6.7% national benchmark, confirming private-market conviction in the format even amid rising rates. KIM leads segment YTD performance at +9.72% while ADC trails at ‑8.54%. Q3 earnings begin October 26 (BRX) with all operating metrics currently Q2 carryforwards.
Regency Read-Through: The Brixmor-Slate deal validates cap-rate levels broadly consistent with REG’s portfolio mark; REG’s record leased occupancy (96.9%), blended +10.4% cash spread, and raised guidance provide a strong Q3 setup, though rate-driven multiple compression could cap near-term price appreciation.
MALL / LIFESTYLE
The Mall/Lifestyle segment outperformed all peers at +0.64% average 5-day return (as of October 2), led by CBL (+5.37%) on continued post-restructuring momentum (the stock is up +47.84% YTD) and MAC (+1.81%). SKT also gained (+1.09%) as outlet traffic remained solid entering the fall selling season. Rate sensitivity for higher-leveraged mall operators remains a risk, but the segment’s YTD average of +15.43% and 1-year average of +22.99% reflect a multi-year recovery narrative that has been durable against rate volatility so far in 2026.
Regency Read-Through: Mall outperformance in volatile weeks reflects opportunistic rotation into recovery/yield names rather than fundamental re-rating; REG’s open-air grocery-anchored model does not face the same format-specific restructuring risk that drove CBL’s discount.
NET LEASE
Net lease was the weakest segment at ‑1.67% average 5-day return (as of October 2), with no positive names in the group. A 10-year yield at or above 5% creates the most direct competitive pressure on long-duration net-lease cash flows, where cap-rate spreads to Treasuries compress most visibly. EPRT (‑2.37% weekly, ‑12.68% YTD) and GTY (‑1.61% weekly) led declines; NNN (‑0.85%) and BNL (‑1.85%) held up relatively better. BNL reports October 28 and NNN November 4.
Regency Read-Through: Net-lease rate sensitivity underscores REG’s structural advantage — shorter effective lease terms, frequent re-leasing events, and grocery-anchor traffic stability reduce duration risk relative to triple-net single-tenant peers. REG’s FFO guidance does not embed the Treasury-spread vulnerability that has compressed net-lease multiples this cycle.
