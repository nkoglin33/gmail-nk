Convenience / Strip
Curbline (CURB) was the standout in a broadly negative week for retail REITs, gaining +0.46% for the five days ending September 25 — the only positive return among grocery-anchored and adjacent names. YTD the stock is up +22.6%, ranking it among the top three in the 19-issuer universe alongside MAC and CBL, driven by continued investor appetite for its pure-play convenience center model and limited supply in the convenience strip sub-market. Q3 2026 earnings are expected October 29.
Regency Read-Through: CURB’s premium YTD re-rating relative to traditional grocery-anchored peers signals that the market values convenience strip density and shorter lease terms as a differentiated value proposition — a format Regency’s core portfolio partially competes with and can benchmark against in smaller-shop rent negotiations.
Grocery-Anchored / Power
The grocery-anchored segment averaged -0.50% for the five days ending September 25 — softer than the prior week but notably more defensive than malls (-1.57%) and net lease (-1.56%). KRG was the segment’s largest laggard at -2.56%, while PECO (+0.62%) and REG (+0.38%) bucked the trend. Institutional investors continued rotating into grocery-anchored assets as the preferred defensive real estate allocation, with cap rates compressing to the low-6% range nationally. Q3 earnings season for this segment kicks off October 26-27 with BRX and PECO reporting.
Regency Read-Through: The sector’s relative defensiveness in a down week validates the grocery-anchored premium and supports Regency’s positioning heading into Q3 results on November 5 — particularly the occupancy-at-record and rent-spread momentum story that was established in Q2.
Mall / Lifestyle
Mall names were the weakest segment for the five-day period ending September 25, averaging -1.57%, led down by CBL (-4.47%) and SKT (-2.99%). Despite the weekly softness, malls lead the 19-issuer universe in YTD (+14.3% avg) and 1-year (+24.3% avg) returns, with CBL (+40.3% YTD) and MAC (+22.6% YTD) driving the average. Truist’s initiation of SPG at Hold with a $215 price target on October 2 reflected a quality-tier thesis: Class A malls remain well-positioned, but secondary exposure limits upside. Upcoming Q3 reports (MAC/BRX Oct 27; SPG/SKT/AKR Oct 28) will provide the first hard read on mall traffic and leasing momentum heading into holiday season.
Regency Read-Through: The mall outperformance in YTD/1-year returns reflects mean reversion from a lower base rather than fundamental competition for Regency’s tenant roster — grocery anchors and open-air centers continue to command operating occupancy premiums that enclosed malls cannot replicate at scale.
Net Lease
Net lease REITs averaged -1.56% for the five days ending September 25, broadly in line with mall weakness. GTY (-3.31%), the sector’s most interest-rate-sensitive name due to its shorter-duration convenience/petroleum asset base, was the largest laggard. EPRT (-10.6% YTD) continues to be pressured by rate sensitivity and portfolio concentration concerns. BNL (+8.9% YTD) and NNN (+4.2% YTD) have held up better, though NNN moved its Q3 report to November 4 (later than prior estimates). ADC’s -6.3% YTD underperformance reflects the market’s concern about single-tenant retail cap rate spreads to Treasuries in a higher-for-longer environment.
Regency Read-Through: Net lease underperformance reinforces the relative-value case for multi-tenant grocery-anchored REITs — shorter average lease terms, diversified tenancy, and embedded rent-step mechanisms make Regency’s cash flow profile more durable than single-tenant net lease in a rate-volatile environment.
