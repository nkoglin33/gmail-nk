Convenience / Strip
Curbline Properties (CURB) extended its 2026 outperformance with a +1.8% week, pushing its year-to-date return to +26.5% — best in the entire 19-name universe. The convenience strip format continues to benefit from near-zero new supply in infill suburban corridors, sustained tenant demand for sub-15,000 sf spaces from grocers, service-oriented tenants, and quick-service restaurants, and CURB's near-zero leverage (net debt/EBITDA of 0.5×) that gives it an acquisition-velocity advantage unavailable to higher-levered peers. CURB's Q2 record investment volume of $375M confirms institutional buyers are pricing convenience assets at sub-6% cap rates with no visible supply-side pressure.
Regency Read-Through: CURB's premium multiple and sub-6% private-market clearing prices underscore the cap-rate compression case for grocery-anchored centers broadly; Regency's ability to create comparable exposure through development at 9% blended yields remains the most compelling deployment alternative while acquisitions are priced at 5.25–5.5%.
Grocery-Anchored / Power
The 9-name composite was roughly flat on the week (+0.2% simple average) as the sector digested a strong earnings run — the last of the direct REG peers (Urban Edge, Aug 6) reported, completing a unanimous Q2 beat-and-raise cycle across the comp group. FRT led at +2.1%, followed by IVT (+1.4%) and UE (+1.0%), while BRX (-0.8%), KIM (-1.2%), and PECO (-0.7%) gave back modest post-earnings gains. The fundamental backdrop remains the strongest in the segment's recent history: record occupancy at multiple names, blended spreads averaging mid-teens, and SP NOI growth clustering between 3.5% and 5.8% across the nine peers. Private capital continues to validate public valuations, with institutional grocery-anchored transaction volume on a record pace at ~$12.8B trailing.
Regency Read-Through: With all 9 direct peers now reported and every one raising guidance, the Q2 earnings cycle eliminates any sector-specific execution concern for Regency; the focus now shifts entirely to REG's own signed-not-open pipeline (69% expected to commence by year-end) as the catalyst for 2027 NOI step-up.
Mall / Lifestyle
The 5-name mall/lifestyle composite was the week's clear laggard, with Macerich's 6.8% selloff on a soft Q2 print (SP NOI +1.9%, leverage 7.9× net debt/EBITDA) dragging the segment sharply negative. Tanger fell 2.9% in sympathy, while Simon held in (-0.5%) supported by its A-rated balance sheet and the expectation of its Q2 report today (August 10). Acadia was modestly lower (-0.4%) despite posting the sector's best Q2 leasing metrics (street-retail SP NOI +8.7%; raised guidance to $1.24–$1.26 full-year FFO). CBL and SKT both reported Q2 results during the week; both showed stable occupancy and modest SP NOI growth but their outlet and value-oriented formats continued to lag grocery-anchored peers in spread momentum.
Regency Read-Through: Mall volatility — and the MAC/SKT selloff on perfectly acceptable underlying results — reinforces the durability premium investors assign to grocery-anchored necessity-based cash flows; the wider the mall/grocery spread gap grows, the clearer the relative value thesis for REG's open-air portfolio becomes.
Net Lease
The 4-name net lease composite drifted lower on the week as the 10-year Treasury held near 4.30% and the Fed remained on hold — reinforcing the rate-sensitivity that is net lease's dominant external variable. NNN REIT (Aug 5) beat Q2 estimates and raised full-year guidance to imply ~3.8% core FFO growth on a 3,711-property portfolio with 10.1-year weighted average lease term. Agree Realty (ADC) slipped -1.1% despite its record Q2 investment volume ($502M), and EPRT was the segment's weakest performer (-1.6%) as middle-market tenant scrutiny from the 2026 bankruptcy cycle (nine retail filings tracked YTD) weighs on sentiment. BNL and GTY were broadly steady. Realty Income (O, not in our universe) disclosed a significant pivot toward private capital for equity sourcing — a template the sector watches closely.
Regency Read-Through: Net lease rate-sensitivity reinforces the structural advantage of Regency's multi-tenant, necessity-anchored lease structure — shorter lease terms and frequent mark-to-market opportunities provide built-in inflation protection that triple-net long-term leases do not, supporting a lower cap rate and higher implied multiple for grocery-anchored open-air.
