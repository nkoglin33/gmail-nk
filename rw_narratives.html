Convenience / Strip
Curbline Properties (CURB) held near its all-time high of $31.89 (set intraday July 20) through the week, supported by Q1 same-property NOI of +4.8% and a June 2026 forward equity offering of ~$308M providing acquisition firepower for its $700M single-asset convenience retail pipeline. Whitestone REIT's NYSE delisting (Ares acquisition at $19.00/share, closed July 14) removes the only other liquid strip/convenience REIT comp, narrowing the investable format universe to CURB as the sole public reference point. Urban infill strip product commands Class A implied cap rates of 5.5%–5.9%, consistent with CURB's trading level and reflecting structural demand for daily-needs, necessity-retail formats.
Regency Read-Through: CURB's all-time high pricing and WSR's Ares exit at a premium validate the scarcity value of well-located necessity-retail strip assets — complementary to Regency's grocery-anchored positioning and consistent with private-market cap rate compression to the mid-5% range for institutional-quality product.
Grocery-Anchored
The grocery-anchored segment entered its Q2 earnings wall as the sector's highest-conviction trade, with all four liquid public names (REG, KIM, KRG, IVT) receiving positive analyst actions and trading near multi-month highs. REG earned four simultaneous price-target increases (Barclays $90, RJ $88, Citi $82, Evercore $80); KRG's Piper Sandler target jumped from $27 to $33; KIM was upgraded at Wells Fargo — all reflecting Q1 SS NOI of +4.4% (REG), +3.6% (KRG), +1.7% (KIM), +2.6% (IVT) and confirmed leasing momentum of +13.5% blended spreads (KRG) and a record 410 bps leased-to-economic spread at KIM representing $77M in future ABR. Institutional capital flowing into private grocery-anchored assets ($12.8B trailing-4-quarter transaction volume, +42% YoY) validates the public sector's fundamental case while a ~145 bps public-private cap rate spread creates implied upside for well-positioned open-air names.
Regency Read-Through: REG's four-analyst consensus price target cluster ($80–$90) and Q1 SS NOI of +4.4% position it as the segment's benchmark quality name; the $600M+ development/redevelopment pipeline at >9% blended returns provides accretive growth well above peer core NOI trajectories entering the July-August reporting window.
Power / Diversified
Agree Realty (ADC) delivered a flat weekly return as investors awaited Q2 results (July 30), though Q1 acquisition velocity of $402.5M at 7.1% initial yields and core FFO growth of +8.1% maintain its investment thesis for a $1.4–$1.6B 2026 buying program at 11.3-year average lease terms. With UBA fully merged into Regency (August 2023) and PREIT navigating post-second-bankruptcy illiquidity on the OTCQB since April 2024, the active public power/diversified segment is effectively a single-name story — ADC providing a high-grade net-lease-adjacent proxy for the format. The 7.1% acquisition yield entry point compares favorably to compressed grocery-anchored private market cap rates (~6.7%), highlighting the risk-return tradeoff between necessity retail formats.
Regency Read-Through: ADC's 7.1% acquisition yields versus Regency's sub-6% implied cap rate confirm the quality premium embedded in grocery-anchored open-air retail; Regency's >9% development/redevelopment blended returns represent an even wider spread versus the acquisition market than any net lease peer can deliver.
Mall / Lifestyle
Simon Property Group (SPG) led the segment with a +2.1% single-day gain on July 24, driven by Goldman Sachs' Buy initiation and a Piper Sandler target increase to $285 (from $230), building on Q1 results showing domestic NOI growth of +6.7%, mall occupancy at 99.2% (+80 bps), and base rent per sq ft up 9.1% within malls on $819/sq ft retailer sales. Macerich (MAC) sustained its +42% YTD gain near the 52-week high of $25.97 set July 17, while Tanger's (SKT) blended rent spreads of +10.5% and retenanting spreads of >+26% confirm outlet center pricing power ahead of August 4 results; Acadia (AKR) and Tanger both approach Q2 reports near multi-year highs. The segment remains bifurcated between institutional-quality trophy mall assets (SPG at 99.2% occupancy) and restructured/recovering names like CBL (+90.4% trailing 52-week) and PREIT (OTC).
Regency Read-Through: SPG's premium mall metrics contrast with open-air outperformance on occupancy cost efficiency — Regency's grocery-anchored tenants carry structurally lower occupancy costs and face less fashion/department-store e-commerce displacement risk than enclosed mall adjacencies, supporting a more durable NOI growth trajectory.
Net Lease
Essential Properties (EPRT) and Getty Realty (GTY) both delivered Q2 2026 beats on July 22 — EPRT posting AFFO of $0.50/share (+9% YoY) with revenue +18.1% on raised investment guidance of $1.2–$1.5B (Stifel issued a $36.25 target), while GTY raised 2026 AFFO guidance for the second consecutive time to $2.52–$2.54/share with Q2 acquisitions at 7.4% initial yields confirming strong deployment momentum. Despite EPRT's fundamental beat, the stock pulled back modestly on profit-taking as the YTD position (-1.8%) reflects earlier-year underperformance. NNN REIT trades at relative value (YTD +17.7%) versus retail REIT peers, with sell-side noting "fully priced" near-term outlook despite a 37th consecutive annual dividend increase; Broadstone (BNL) approaches Q2 with Hold consensus, AFFO growing +5.6%, and a recapture rate of 119% on lease renewals.
Regency Read-Through: Net lease acquisition cap rates of 7.1–8.2% versus grocery-anchored private market ~6.7% highlight the relative yield compression in anchored open-air retail — institutional demand validates Regency's ability to command premium pricing for necessity-driven, multi-tenant open-air assets over single-tenant net lease formats.
