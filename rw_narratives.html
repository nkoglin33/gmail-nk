Convenience / Strip
Curbline Properties (CURB), the segment's sole member, continues to trade as a premium within the open-air universe — +27.6% YTD through the last confirmed close (Aug 7) reflects investor appetite for its pure-play convenience-format narrative following the 2024 spin from Site Centers. Q2 2026 results showed 96.5% leased (+20bps sequential), full-year OFFO guidance of $1.24–$1.26, and SP NOI growth of +3.1% excluding storm damage. Q3 reporting is expected in late October 2026; the key watch items are whether occupancy pushes toward 97% and whether the storm-impacted NOI drag (~360bps in Q2) reverses in H2. With the ex-dividend dates and quiet periods approaching across the sector, CURB enters Q3 earnings season in a strong fundamental position relative to its convenience-format peers.
Regency Read-Through: CURB's outperformance validates market demand for small-bay, convenience-oriented formats — the same tenant mix anchoring Regency's own small-shop leasing pipeline. Strong CURB results support higher renewal spread assumptions for comparable inline formats in REG's portfolio.
Grocery-Anchored / Power
The 9-ticker segment averaged -4.4% in the last confirmed trading week (ending Aug 7) but remains up an average of +14.2% YTD, grounded in exceptional Q2 fundamentals: PECO hit 97.3% leased (record), KIM hit 96.4% (record), and REG reached 96.9% (+40bps YoY). With Q3 2026 ending September 30, management teams are now entering their quiet periods; no incremental guidance updates or operational color are expected before the October earnings window. Price data for this cycle remains unavailable due to the ongoing API proxy block (fourth consecutive week). The key Q3 watch items are SNO pipeline conversion pace (REG $41M, BRX $71M are the two largest in the comp set) and whether back-to-school foot traffic at grocery-anchored centers is tracking consistent with the positive tone from Q2 calls.
Regency Read-Through: REG's 3.8% SP NOI growth and $41M SNO pipeline position the company for continued NOI acceleration in H2 2026 as leases commence. The peer-wide beat-and-raise Q2 cycle removes sector execution overhang and creates a cleaner path to multiple re-rating in Q3 earnings season.
Mall / Lifestyle
The Mall/Lifestyle segment delivered the strongest YTD performance of the four segments (+24.7% avg through Aug 7), led by CBL's post-emergence recovery story (+52.8% YTD) and Macerich's luxury-repositioning thesis (+32.2% YTD). SPG held near its all-time high following its Q2 beat (FFO +7.9%, domestic NOI +8.5%, occupancy 96.0%); Deutsche Bank's July downgrade to Hold ($220 target) on valuation has not materially weighed on the stock. AKR remains the segment laggard at +0.5% YTD, reflecting its urban street-retail concentration. Q3 reports for mall names are expected in late October through early November 2026. Back-to-school retail traffic data through early September is a key early read on the Q3 consumer environment.
Regency Read-Through: Continued mall sector strength signals healthy consumer engagement broadly, though Regency's grocery-anchored open-air format is structurally differentiated from enclosed and premium-outlet formats. Mall traffic trends in Q3 provide a useful read on back-to-school and early holiday demand cadence.
Net Lease
The Net Lease segment was the relative outperformer in the last confirmed trading period (avg -2.0% vs. -4.4% for grocery-anchored), reflecting its long-duration, contracted-income profile. GTY (+22.0% YTD) and BNL (+20.5% YTD) have been the segment's best performers through Aug 7; NNN sits at +18.2% YTD. EPRT continues to underperform at +3.3% YTD, weighed by acquisition-volume concerns and elevated leverage relative to NNN and BNL. The 10-year Treasury near 4.3% remains a modest cap-rate headwind; one Q4 Fed cut is still priced by markets, which could provide a near-term tailwind to net-lease valuations if realized. Q3 reports are expected beginning in late October (GTY Oct 23, EPRT Oct 28, BNL Oct 29, NNN Nov 3).
Regency Read-Through: Net lease's relatively defensive YTD profile signals that markets continue to reward long-duration cash flow certainty. The spread between grocery-anchored implied cap rates (~6.0%) and net-lease (~6.5%–7.0%) has compressed — reinforcing the relative valuation case for quality open-air assets going into Q3 earnings.
